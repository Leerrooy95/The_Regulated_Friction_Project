## Summary

The SAVE America Act (H.R. 7296) evolved from a voter ID bill into a mechanism for centralizing all 50 states' unredacted voter registration data under the Department of Homeland Security — with no restrictions on what DHS can do with that data once received and no safeguards against using it to force voter purges or question election results. The DHS database (SAVE — Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements) has already been documented as error-prone, mistakenly flagging hundreds of U.S. citizens as noncitizens in Texas, Missouri, Arizona, and other states. Citizens flagged by SAVE are also referred to DHS for possible criminal investigation.

The bill passed the House 218-213 on February 11, 2026. The Senate opened debate 51-48 on March 17. The bill lacks the 60 votes needed for cloture and is expected to fail — but its interaction with the FISA Section 702 expiration on April 20 creates a third convergence track inside the April 12-20 prediction window already filed in this repository.

## The Legislative Evolution

### From SAVE Act to SAVE America Act

| Version | Year | Core Requirement | What It Added |
|---------|------|-----------------|---------------|
| SAVE Act (H.R. 22) | 2024-2025 | Documentary proof of citizenship to register to vote | Passed House April 2025; died in Senate |
| SAVE America Act (H.R. 7296) | 2026 | Same citizenship proof requirement PLUS photo ID at polls PLUS states must share unredacted voter rolls with DHS | Passed House 218-213 (Feb 11, 2026); Senate debate opened 51-48 (March 17) |

**Key additions in the 2026 version:**
- States must submit complete, unredacted voter registration lists to DHS for comparison against the SAVE database
- Photo ID required at polls AND for mail-in voting (copies must accompany absentee ballots)
- In-person registration required for documentary proof — online and mail registration systems would need overhaul
- Criminal penalties for election officials who register someone without proper documentation (up to 5 years)
- Private right of action allowing anyone to sue election officials for registration violations
- No restrictions on what the federal government can do with voter data once DHS receives it
- No safeguards against using the data for voter purges or to question election results
- No additional federal funding allocated to implement the new requirements

## The DHS SAVE Database: Documented Failures

The Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) database was originally designed to check immigrants' eligibility for public benefits — not to mass-verify voter citizenship. Trump's March 2025 executive order directed DHS to give states free access and partnered with DOGE to comb voter rolls. DHS rushed the expanded system into use.

### Root Cause

DHS rushed the revamped SAVE tool into use while it was still adding data. The system particularly struggles with naturalized citizens — people born outside the U.S. who later became citizens. SAVE checks Social Security Administration records first, but SSA has historically relied on people to voluntarily report citizenship changes in person, creating significant data lags.

USCIS verification division lead Brian Broderick acknowledged the system can't always find current citizenship information for people not born in the U.S., but stated it was "ultimately up to states to decide how to use SAVE data."

### The Criminal Referral Problem

When SAVE flags voters as noncitizens, they are also referred to DHS for possible criminal investigation. This means naturalized citizens who were incorrectly flagged by a broken database face not only potential disenfranchisement but criminal referral — for the crime of being a citizen who registered to vote.

### Scale of the "Problem" Being Solved

At least seven states with ~35 million registered voters ran their rolls through SAVE. Total noncitizens identified: ~4,200 — approximately **0.01%** of registered voters. This aligns with previous findings that noncitizen voting is rare. Even this number includes the documented false positives.

## The Data Centralization Architecture

Under the SAVE America Act, DHS would receive:
- Unredacted voter registration lists from all 50 states
- Name, address, date of birth, SSN (last 4), driver's license number for every registered voter
- Ongoing access for "continuous verification" — not a one-time check

The bill contains:
- **No restrictions** on what DHS does with the data after receiving it
- **No safeguards** against using the data to force voter purges
- **No safeguards** against using the data to challenge election results
- **No sunset provision** on DHS data retention
- **No independent audit requirement** for SAVE accuracy

The DHS Secretary position — which would oversee this entire voter data infrastructure — is currently being filled by Markwayne Mullin, who advanced from committee 8-7 on March 19, 2026. During his confirmation hearing, Mullin refused to rule out deploying ICE to polling locations.

27 states have already agreed to use SAVE voluntarily. The SAVE America Act would make it mandatory for all 50.
