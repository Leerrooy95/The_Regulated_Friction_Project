## The Procedural Mechanics — What the Shift Eliminates

The distinction between formal hearings and roundtables is procedural, not informal. Each procedural mechanism the shift removes corresponds to a previously-functioning accountability tool:

| Formal Hearing Element | Roundtable Equivalent | Effect of Removal |
|------------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Witnesses sworn under oath | No oath | Witnesses cannot be charged with perjury for false statements; testimony does not enter evidentiary record with sworn-statement weight |
| Recorded committee votes | No votes possible | Committee members cannot force votes on subpoenas, contempt referrals, or document demands during the proceeding |
| Subpoena power exercised at hearing | No subpoenas possible | Bipartisan member coalitions cannot force-issue subpoenas through floor motions |
| Formal congressional record | No formal record | Statements made in roundtable do not become part of permanent Congressional Record; cannot be cited as testimony in subsequent legal proceedings with the same authority |
| Five-minute member rounds with member-controlled questioning | Chairman-controlled "orderly conversation" format | Individual members lose independent questioning authority; chair controls flow |
| Mandatory monthly committee meetings (House Rule XI Clause 2(b)) | Compliance ambiguous | Garcia memo argues Comer's pattern violates House rules; not yet adjudicated |
| Bipartisan motion authority | None | The mechanism through which 7 motions / 18 subpoenas were forced since July 2025 is eliminated |

[Inference] The Garcia memo's framing — "Oversight Republicans are avoiding hearings to block bipartisan subpoena motions they are losing" — is the most parsimonious explanation. The shift is observed to begin precisely after the bipartisan subpoena motions had become regularly successful and shortly after Bondi's April 2 firing reduced the administration's Epstein-track exposure on the executive branch side. Causal coordination is interpretive; correlation in timing is documented.

## What This Document Does NOT Claim

- ❌ No claim that the roundtable shift is itself illegal under House rules. The Garcia memo argues a violation of House Rule XI Clause 2(b) (monthly meeting requirement) but this has not been adjudicated, and the rule allows for chair flexibility on format.
- ❌ No claim that the roundtable format is unprecedented in House history. Roundtable formats have been used previously for non-controversial topics. The novelty is the systematic substitution for hearings on subjects where bipartisan subpoena motions had been succeeding.
- ❌ No claim that the White House directly ordered the shift. The Garcia, Stansbury, and Crockett attributions are documented; direct evidence of presidential or White House staff direction is not in the public record.
- ❌ No claim that Speaker Johnson directly ordered the shift. Crockett's attribution is documented; direct evidence is not in the public record. Comer denies White House direction but does not address Speaker Johnson direction.
- ❌ No claim that the timing convergence (Bondi firing + Melania statement + Bilderberg + Zampolli SLAPP + roundtable consolidation) constitutes coordination. The convergence window framing in `tier3_thermostat_ruleset.md` describes structural emergence of compliance behavior, not coordination.
- ❌ No claim that any of the scheduled roundtable subjects (Lutnick, Gates, Waitt, Noel) are themselves implicated in Epstein's criminal conduct. The roundtable format affects the testimonial process regardless of subject status.
- ❌ No claim that survivor hearings will not occur. Comer has stated they will. The framework observation is that they are sequenced after roundtable interviews of high-profile subjects, deferring the formal record into a later news cycle.
