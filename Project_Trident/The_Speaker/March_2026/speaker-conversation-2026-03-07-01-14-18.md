### GAP 3: Section 122 Tariff Cliff — The July 24 Deadline

*Researching what actually happens when Section 122 expires and how markets/Gulf SWFs are positioning*

**Key Findings:**

The Section 122 situation is more complex and consequential than the KB captures. Here is the full picture:

**The legal architecture:** Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2132) allows the President to impose a 15% import surcharge for up to 150 days to address a "large and serious" balance-of-payments deficit. The statute explicitly states Congressional action is required to extend beyond 150 days. The 150-day clock from the February 20 SCOTUS IEEPA ruling puts the hard stop at approximately July 19-24, 2026.

**What Congress won't do:** Speaker Johnson's "unlikely consensus" statement on February 23 was effectively a legislative veto. The House Republican conference is split between free-trade establishment Republicans (who opposed the tariffs on principle) and MAGA protectionists (who support them). The Senate is equally divided. No clean tariff codification bill has the votes.

**Alternative mechanisms being explored:**

*Option 1 — Section 232 (National Security):* The President can invoke Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act for national security-justified tariffs. These are not subject to the same 150-day limit. However, SCOTUS has never directly ruled on Section 232 limits, and the same 6-3 IEEPA majority might scrutinize Section 232 overuse.

*Option 2 — Section 301 (Unfair Trade Practices):* USTR can investigate and impose tariffs under Section 301 against specific countries for unfair trade practices. These require an investigation period (typically 12 months) but can be expedited. China-specific tariffs from the first Trump term survived under this authority.

*Option 3 — Negotiated bilateral deals:* Trump could convert the 15% global surcharge into negotiated bilateral reductions — essentially using the tariff cliff as leverage to extract bilateral concessions before the deadline. This would replace a global tariff with country-specific deals, politically claiming "victory" while technically complying with the SCOTUS ruling.

**How Gulf SWFs appear to be positioning:** The KB documents Mubadala's Q4 2025 13F showed zero defense positions and increased Bitcoin accumulation. The Section 122 expiration creates specific capital flow implications:

**Market implications:** The July 24 deadline is creating a 4-month uncertainty window for any supply chain-dependent sector. Capital is likely flowing toward tariff-insulated investments (domestic services, real estate, infrastructure) and away from tariff-sensitive manufacturing. This is directly relevant to the QXO building products consolidation — construction materials are largely domestic supply chains.
