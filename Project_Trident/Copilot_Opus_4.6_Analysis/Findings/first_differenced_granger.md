**First-differencing DESTROYS all Granger causality in the event-count

## Stationarity Test Results (Augmented Dickey-Fuller)

### Event-count dataset (1,879 weeks)

| Series | ADF Statistic | p-value | Result |
|--------|---------------|---------|--------|
| Friction (levels) | -14.587 | < 0.0001 | ✅ Stationary |
| Compliance (levels) | -5.773 | < 0.0001 | ✅ Stationary |
| ΔFriction | -22.148 | < 0.0001 | ✅ Stationary |
| ΔCompliance | -24.611 | < 0.0001 | ✅ Stationary |

Both level series pass the ADF test, which means the Granger test's
stationarity assumption is technically satisfied. Despite this, the
level-based Granger results are driven by shared sparse-data patterns
(many zero-count weeks) rather than genuine predictive relationships.

### 30-row hand-scored dataset

| Series | ADF Statistic | p-value | Result |
|--------|---------------|---------|--------|
| Friction Index (levels) | 0.786 | 0.991 | ⚠️ **Non-stationary** |
| Compliance Index (levels) | -2.770 | 0.063 | ⚠️ Non-stationary (marginal) |
| ΔFriction Index | -1.499 | 0.534 | ⚠️ Non-stationary |
| ΔCompliance Index | -2.998 | 0.035 | ✅ Stationary |

The hand-scored Friction Index is **clearly non-stationary** (p = 0.991).
This means the level-based Granger results on this dataset formally
violate the stationarity assumption — yet remarkably, the F→C finding
**survives differencing** (see below).

## Granger Causality Comparison: Levels vs First Differences

### Event-count dataset (1,879 weeks)

| Direction | LEVELS | FIRST DIFFERENCES |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| Friction → Compliance | ✅ Lags 1-2 | ✗ **Nothing** |
| Compliance → Friction | ✅ Lags 1-3, 7-8 | ✗ **Nothing** |

**Conclusion:** The event-count Granger causality is entirely an artifact.
When we ask "does a *change* in friction predict a *change* in compliance?"
the answer is NO in both directions.

### 30-row hand-scored dataset (30 observations)

| Direction | LEVELS | FIRST DIFFERENCES |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| Friction → Compliance | ✅ Lags 1-2 (p=0.0008, 0.027) | ✅ **Lags 1-2 (p=0.024, 0.039)** |
| Compliance → Friction | ✅ Lag 1 (p=0.022) | ⚠️ Lag 2 only (p=0.028) |

**Conclusion:** The hand-scored F→C finding SURVIVES first-differencing.
A *change* in friction intensity predicts a *change* in compliance intensity
1-2 weeks later. The reverse direction weakens and shifts to lag 2 only.

### What this means for the thesis:

The strongest statistical support for the friction→compliance sequential
mechanism comes from the **30-row hand-scored dataset** — where Austin
manually rated friction and compliance intensity. In that dataset, changes
in friction intensity genuinely predict changes in compliance intensity
1-2 weeks later, even after stationarity correction.

The event-count dataset does NOT support this — but this may be because
raw event counts are too coarse to capture the intensity nuances that
the hand-scored indices measure. See Task 3 (hand-scored vs event-count
discrepancy investigation) for a deeper analysis of why.
