## Interpretation

### What survived:

1. **Dec 2025 removal:** The bidirectional pattern is fully stable. F→C remains
   significant at lags 1-2, C→F at lags 1-3 and 7-8. December 2025 is not
   driving the Granger result.

2. **Nov-Dec 2025 removal:** F→C weakens to lag 1 only (p=0.010), but C→F
   remains significant at 4 lags. The pattern becomes more asymmetric (C→F
   dominant), which aligns more with the "compliance predicts friction" finding
   from the prior archived analysis.

### What did NOT survive:

3. **All 2025 excluded:** Complete collapse. Neither direction shows any
   significance at any lag. This is the critical finding — **the Granger
   causality signal is entirely a 2025 phenomenon.** It does not exist in
   the pre-2025 timeline.

4. **Pre-2020 and 2020-2024:** Both sub-periods show zero Granger causality.
   The signal is concentrated entirely in 2025 data.

### 30-row master CSV:

The hand-scored 30-row dataset still shows F→C at lags 1-2 (p=0.0008, p=0.027)
with only weak C→F at lag 1 (p=0.022). This remains the strongest evidence for
the thesis direction, but the sample size (30 rows) limits confidence.

## Does This Change Prior Conclusions?

**Partially.** The prior Granger results document stated the direction was
bidirectional for the full event-count dataset. That conclusion is **confirmed
and robust to Dec 2025 removal**.

However, the new finding that **all Granger causality disappears without 2025
data** is significant. It means:

1. The predictive relationship between friction and compliance only emerged
   (or became detectable) in 2025
2. This could reflect: (a) 2025 events genuinely having a different structure,
   (b) dataset coverage being denser in 2025, or (c) both
3. The 30-row hand-scored data (which covers a specific ~30-week window) may
   capture a real but temporally limited phenomenon

**This does NOT invalidate the finding** — 2025 is the period of interest for
the thesis. But it means the Granger result should be described as "a 2025
phenomenon" rather than "a persistent multi-year pattern."
