## 4. Background Context (Brief)

This research project tracks a pattern it calls "regulated friction" — the idea that political scandals, document releases, and media crises tend to cluster before major policy or financial changes. The headline statistical finding is a correlation of **r = 0.6196** (p = 0.0004, n = 28 paired observations) between "friction" events and "compliance" events at a two-week lag. This means friction events tend to predict compliance events about 7 days later. The project tracks 14 active "leverage nodes" — entities that hold or are subject to information, capital, or kinetic leverage in current geopolitics. In early 2026, the project retracted Layers 2 and 3 of its original framework after three key predictions failed (EA position stability, Mubadala defense expansion, and Gulf SWF Oracle/defense entries).
