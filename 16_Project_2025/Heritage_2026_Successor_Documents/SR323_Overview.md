# Heritage SR323 — Overview ("Project 2026" shorthand)

**Source:** Heritage Foundation Special Report No. 323, *Saving America by Saving the Family: A Foundation for the Next 250 Years*, Jan 8, 2026. DeVos Center for Human Flourishing. Authors: Roger Severino, Jay W. Richards, Emma Waters, Delano Squires, Rachel Sheffield, Robert Rector.
**Line references** (`L####`; section ranges below are approximate, taken from headings) point to the text-extracted copy of the report (6,928 lines; body ends ~L5535, endnotes follow). Report page numbers are given only where a footer was seen.
**Naming note:** "Project 2026" is a media/critic nickname. Heritage has said there is no "Project 2026"; SR323 describes itself as the first in a series of special reports. This folder uses the report's own name.

## TL;DR
- A ~130-page family-policy blueprint. Thesis (L34–L40): restoring "the family home" is a matter of justice; the ideal is "a loving and stable home with their married biological parents."
- Three imperatives: (1) stop penalizing marriage/family formation, (2) restore the American Dream (economic conditions for family formation), (3) actively support marriage and working families.
- Three headline federal mechanisms (L343–L393): **NEST accounts**, a **FAM credit** (adoption-credit-sized credit for married parents' newborns), and a **Home Childcare Equalization (HCE) credit**. Plus a President-level ask: executive orders requiring every federal action to measure effects on marriage and family (L322).
- Time horizon is explicitly long-term (L5205–L5223: policies "adopted now" whose payoff "will likely not be seen for many years"), anchored to July 4, 2026 (the 250th) in the intro (L43).
- The report is a mix of: (a) federal asks, (b) state-level ideas (custody, alimony, covenant marriage, blue laws), (c) civil-society asks (churches, colleges, employers), (d) normative principles (conclusion, L5271–L5310), and (e) large amounts of empirical and causal argument.

## The three federal mechanisms
1. **NEST (Newlywed Early Starters Trust).** Summary (L355–L362): $2,500 initial deposit distributed over three years upon eligible marriage by about age 30; "more than $38,000 by age 30" if married by 28; unclaimed funds convert to IRAs. Detailed version (L4935+): seeded with ≥$2,500 at a child's birth, no withdrawals until marriage or age 30, phased tax-free release (33% / 50% / 100% over three married tax years). Cost: ~$92B/10yr in the text (L5188); appendix mid estimate −$83.8B (L5440). See Inconsistencies file.
2. **FAM credit.** $4,418 refundable per year for 4 years ($17,670 total, equal to the adoption credit max) for married joint filers with biological newborn; work requirement; +25% Large Family Bonus from 3rd child; divorce forfeits (L4690–L4760).
3. **Home Childcare Equalization (HCE).** +$2,000 per child under 5 for married FAM-eligible families; offset dollar-for-dollar by CDCTC/CCDF (L4809–L4856). FAM + HCE: $188.7B/10yr (L4861).

## Cost framing
FAM+HCE+NEST ≈ "less than nine-tenths of a percent" of projected Social Security + Medicare spending, "$116 on Social Security and Medicare for each dollar" (L5224–L5229). Offsets from welfare/fraud reductions are asserted but not scored.

## Welfare asks (L2312–L2380)
Remove marriage penalties across ~90 means-tested programs; strengthen TANF/housing/SNAP work requirements; restructure and simplify; cap stacked benefits; cut EITC improper payments. Evidence offered: modeled marriage-penalty example (L2232–L2246), EITC filer vs single-parent-family count mismatch (L2358–L2367), $1.68T/yr means-tested spending.

## Reading cautions
- The report mixes proposals with advocacy and contains internal inconsistencies (see `SR323_Internal_Inconsistencies.md`).
- Several figures are modeled illustrations, not observed outcomes (e.g., the marriage-penalty example assumes the father's income is "off the books" when unmarried).
- Some "asks" are implicit (critiques that imply a fix) or retrospective (endorsing actions already taken). The register flags these.
